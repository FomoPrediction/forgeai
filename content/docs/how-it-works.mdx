## What a machine-hour is

The unit the whole system is priced in. One machine, working, for one hour,
billed to a customer.

An hour was either worked and billed or it was not, so the revenue that reaches
stakers ties back to specific hours.

## What sets the rate

<Split>
  <Side title="Utilisation" hue="copper">
    <p>The share of the fleet that is actually booked. An idle machine bills
    nothing, so a quiet month is a lower rate rather than a subsidised one.</p>
  </Side>
  <Side title="Price per hour" hue="aqua">
    <p>What the work is worth. Different tasks and different hardware bill at
    different rates, and the mix moves the blended figure.</p>
  </Side>
</Split>

The published rate is trailing: what was collected, divided by what was
staked.

## What is onchain and what is not

A machine is a physical object in a building, and the hours it works are
recorded by the systems that operate it.

| Onchain | Offchain |
| --- | --- |
| Stakes, positions and shares | The fleet, and its maintenance |
| Distributions to stakers | Customer contracts and billing |
| Task receipts | Operating the machines |
| Governance parameters | Collection |

The chain settles who is owed what.

## Risks worth naming

<Callout title="The rate moves with the work" type="warn">
A quiet quarter pays less. A fleet that loses a customer pays less. The rate
follows billed hours.
</Callout>

<Callout title="Withdrawals are not always instant">
Capital backing machines that are mid-contract cannot leave on demand.
Withdrawals run through a queue, and the app shows an estimate against the
current book before you commit.
</Callout>

## The data programme

Separate from staking, and worth not confusing with it. The
[task programme](/docs/tasks) records robotics training data and mints a receipt
for every verified submission. Contribution is scored in
[Copper](/docs/copper).

It runs alongside staking and shares the protocol's treasury.
